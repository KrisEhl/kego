19th vs classical ML on two Kaggle playgrounds

    Every method gets the same raw columns and is scored on a fixed holdout that no model trained, tuned or early-stopped on. 19th in standard mode, one run per training size; classical models are LightGBM, logistic regression and Optuna-tuned XGBoost, averaged over seeds.

    Kaggle public leaderboard (card fraud)

    Scored by Kaggle on its own test set. XGBoost was tuned on 599,960 rows and refit on all 699,960 for the submission; 19th trained on 599,960. Public leaders score about 0.971 with feature engineering that no model here used.

    Method and caveats
    • Training samples are nested: each smaller sample is part of every larger one. Card-fraud samples and holdout are stratified (1.67% fraud).
    • Holdouts: 1,000 rows for heart disease, 100,000 rows (1,670 frauds) for card fraud.
    • The lead is a paired bootstrap of 19th's AUC minus the best classical model's AUC (seed-averaged predictions). A filled dot means the 95% interval excludes zero.
    • XGBoost is tuned per training size with Optuna (60 trials or 15 minutes) on an 80/20 split of that sample; on very small samples the tuning split is too small and it scores poorly.
    • 19th's instant, high and ultra modes, and its faster Turbo and Warpspeed model variants, were also run on card fraud. All scored within about 0.002 AUC of standard mode; see dashboard_fraud.html.
    • One 19th run per size; no feature engineering for any method.
